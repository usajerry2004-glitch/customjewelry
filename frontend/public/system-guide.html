What is JewelFlow OS?
JewelFlow OS is a custom jewelry management system built for Kira Jewels. It tracks every custom jewelry order from the moment it's placed through design, manufacturing, and delivery — keeping everyone on the team aligned and every customer informed.

The core problem it solves

Custom jewelry involves many people — sales reps, designers, factory managers, shipping teams, and customers — all working together over weeks or months. Without a system, things get lost, communication breaks down, and customers are left in the dark. JewelFlow OS puts every order, every file, every message, and every status update in one place that everyone can access.

The Big Picture

📥
Order Comes In
A customer or sales rep places a custom jewelry order with all the details — metal type, stone preferences, size, design inspiration. The order enters the system and waits for review.
✏️
Design & Approval
CAD designers create a 3D digital design of the piece. The customer reviews it, suggests changes, and approves. Nothing goes to manufacturing until the customer says yes.
🚚
Made & Delivered
Once approved, the piece goes to the factory in India. After quality checks, it's shipped with a tracking number. The customer is updated every step of the way.

The Complete Order Journey
From the first conversation to the moment the jewelry arrives at the customer's door — here is every step an order goes through, explained in plain language.
STEP 1
1
📥 Order Intake
Order is Placed
A customer or sales rep creates a new order with all the details: what type of jewelry, which metal, stone preferences, size, and any special design notes. A unique PO number is assigned automatically.
👤 Who: Customer (self-service) or Sales Rep
STEP 2
2
✅ Authorization
Order is Reviewed & Authorized
An Authorizer (senior team member) reviews the order for completeness and feasibility. They can generate an AI summary to quickly understand the order. Once satisfied, they authorize it — which releases it to the design team.
👤 Who: Authorizer or Admin
STEP 3
3
✏️ CAD Design
3D Design is Created
The CAD Designer creates a detailed 3D digital model (called a CAD file) of the jewelry piece using professional software. The file shows every dimension, prong position, stone placement, and surface detail. They upload this to the order along with their design notes.
👤 Who: CAD Designer
STEP 4
4
👁️ Design Review
Customer Reviews the Design
The CAD file is sent to the customer for approval. The customer can view the design right in their browser (no special software needed). They can: Approve it, Request Changes with feedback, or Reject it. Every action they take is automatically recorded in the conversation thread.
👤 Who: Customer (via Customer Portal)
STEP 4b
↺
🔄 Revision Loop (if needed)
Revisions Until Perfect
If the customer requests changes, the CAD Designer makes revisions and uploads a new version (Rev #2, Rev #3, etc.). Each revision is kept in the system so the full design history is visible. This loop continues until the customer approves.
👤 Who: CAD Designer → Customer
STEP 5
5
🏷️ SKU & Pricing
SKU Number is Assigned
Once the design is approved, the SKU Manager creates an internal product code (SKU) for the piece. This code identifies the exact specifications of the item and is used throughout manufacturing and inventory. A price quote is also finalized at this stage.
👤 Who: SKU Manager
STEP 6
6
🏭 Manufacturing
Order Goes to Factory
A Vendor Purchase Order (VPO) is issued to the factory in India. The Factory Manager tracks the piece as it goes through casting, stone setting, polishing, and quality inspection. A physical "Job Bag" accompanies the piece through every step of manufacturing.
👤 Who: Factory Manager
STEP 7
7
📦 Shipping
Invoiced, Packed & Shipped
Once the piece passes quality inspection, it's marked Ready to Invoice. The Shipping Manager generates the invoice, packs the order, and dispatches it with a tracking number. The tracking number is entered into the system so the customer can follow their package.
👤 Who: Shipping Manager
STEP 8
✓
🎁 Complete
Delivered — Order Complete
The order is marked Delivered. The customer has their piece. The full record — every design revision, every message, every status change — remains in the system permanently for reference.
👤 Who: Shipping Manager confirms delivery
Team Roles
JewelFlow OS has 8 different roles. Each person only sees and can do what's relevant to their job. Here's what each role does and what they're responsible for.
👑
Admin
Full Access
The administrator has access to everything in the system. They can see all orders, all financial data (including quoted costs and revenue), manage users, and perform any action any other role can do.
  • View all orders across every stage
  • See revenue and quoted cost data (hidden from other roles)
  • Create and manage staff accounts
  • Move orders through any status
  • Access the full order pipeline and analytics dashboard
  • Generate AI summaries of any order
🤝
Sales Rep
Order Entry
Sales reps are the first point of contact. They take orders from stores or clients and enter all the details into the system. They're the bridge between the customer and the internal team.
  • Create new orders on behalf of customers or stores
  • Enter all jewelry specifications (metal, stone, size, etc.)
  • Upload reference images and customer notes
  • Communicate with customers through the order conversation
  • Track the status of their orders
⚖️
Authorizer
Quality Gate
The Authorizer is a senior team member who reviews new orders before they're released to the design team. They make sure the order is complete, feasible, and correctly priced before work begins.
  • Review new orders waiting for authorization
  • Generate and read AI order briefs for quick understanding
  • Approve orders to release them to the CAD team
  • Flag or reject orders that need more information
  • Approve or reject completed CAD designs
✏️
CAD Designer
Design
CAD Designers create the 3D digital models of each jewelry piece. They use specialized software to design every detail — then upload the files to the order so the customer can review them.
  • See only orders in the design stages (their queue)
  • Upload CAD design files (images, PDFs, 3D files)
  • Add designer notes explaining the design decisions
  • Send designs to customers for approval
  • Make revisions based on customer feedback
  • Mark orders ready to move to SKU/manufacturing
🏷️
SKU Manager
Catalog
The SKU Manager handles the internal product catalog. After a design is approved, they create a unique product code (SKU) that identifies the exact piece and its specifications throughout manufacturing.
  • Create SKU numbers for approved designs
  • Manage the product catalog and specifications
  • Issue Vendor Purchase Orders (VPOs) to the factory
  • Maintain inventory records
  • Move orders from design to manufacturing
🏭
Factory Manager
Manufacturing
The Factory Manager oversees production at the manufacturing facility in India. They track each piece as it moves through casting, stone setting, polishing, and quality inspection.
  • View all orders in the manufacturing queue
  • Create physical Job Bags that travel with each piece
  • Track orders through each manufacturing stage
  • Mark orders complete when quality inspection passes
  • Coordinate with contractors and setters
  • Move orders to Ready to Invoice when finished
🚚
Shipping Manager
Fulfillment
The Shipping Manager handles the final stage — getting the finished piece to the customer. They generate invoices, arrange packaging, dispatch shipments, and update tracking information.
  • View orders that are ready to ship
  • Generate invoices and packing slips
  • Enter tracking numbers when shipments are dispatched
  • Mark orders as Shipped and Delivered
  • Handle any shipping issues or returns
💍
Customer
Self-Service
Customers have their own private portal where they can place orders, track progress, review CAD designs, and communicate with the Kira Jewels team — all without needing to email or call.
  • Place new custom jewelry orders anytime
  • Upload reference images and inspiration photos
  • View their orders and track progress with a timeline
  • View CAD design files directly in the browser
  • Approve or request changes to designs
  • Chat with the team through the order conversation
  • See only their own orders — complete privacy
Order Stages Explained
Every order in the system has a status that tells you exactly where it is in the process. Here's what each status means in plain language, and who is responsible at that stage.
Waiting Confirmation
A new order has just come in. It's sitting in the inbox, waiting for an Authorizer to review it and give the green light to proceed.
→ Authorizer
Pending CAD
The order has been authorized. The CAD design team has been notified and the order is in their queue, waiting to be picked up for design work.
→ CAD Designer
CAD In Progress
A designer is actively working on the 3D model. They have uploaded at least one design file and it may be going through internal review or revisions before being sent to the customer.
→ CAD Designer
Customer Approved
🎉 The customer has seen the design and approved it. The piece is exactly what they want. Now it moves forward to SKU creation and manufacturing.
→ SKU Manager
Customer Rejected
The customer has rejected the design — not just requesting changes, but a full rejection. The team needs to go back to the drawing board and restart the design process.
→ CAD Designer
SKU Creation
The SKU Manager is creating the internal product code for this piece. This code will follow the item through manufacturing and inventory forever.
→ SKU Manager
VPO Issued
A Vendor Purchase Order has been sent to the factory in India. The factory has received the instruction to begin making this piece. Manufacturing is starting.
→ Factory Manager
Pending Contractor
This piece requires specialist work — such as a stone setter, engraver, or specific technique — and is waiting for a contractor to be assigned and begin work.
→ Factory Manager
Job Bag Created
A physical job bag (a document envelope with all specifications) has been created at the factory and is traveling with the piece through production. The item is actively being made.
→ Factory Manager
Ready to Invoice
The piece has passed quality inspection at the factory and is ready to be invoiced and shipped. The Shipping Manager is next in line to process it.
→ Shipping Manager
Ready to Ship
The invoice has been generated and the piece is packed, labelled, and sitting ready to be dispatched. Just waiting for collection by the courier.
→ Shipping Manager
Shipped
The package is on its way! A tracking number has been entered into the system. The customer can see the tracking number in their portal and follow the delivery.
→ In Transit
Delivered
✅ The customer has received their piece. The order is complete. All records are preserved permanently.
→ Complete

How to Place an Order
Orders can be placed two ways: by the customer themselves through the Customer Portal, or by a Sales Rep on behalf of a client. Here's how both work.

💍 Customer Self-Service

The customer logs into their portal at any time and places the order themselves. No phone call needed.

Best for: regular customers who know what they want

🤝 Sales Rep Assisted

A Sales Rep speaks with the client, gathers all the details, and enters the order on their behalf through the staff portal.

Best for: first-time customers, complex orders, trade clients

Step-by-Step: What Information is Collected

1

Order Type

What type of jewelry? Engagement Ring, Wedding Band, Necklace, Earrings, Bracelet, Pendant, Brooch, or Other. This sets the context for everything that follows.

2

Metal Selection

Choose the metal type (10K, 14K, 18K, Platinum, Sterling Silver) and color (Yellow Gold, White Gold, Rose Gold, Platinum, Two-Tone). This determines the material cost and appearance of the piece.

3

Ring Size (if applicable)

For rings and bracelets, a size is required. For rings, this is typically a US size (e.g., 6, 6.5, 7). The CAD designer will build the piece to this exact size.

4

Stone Details

The center stone type (Certified Lab Grown Diamond, Non Certified CVD, Non Certified HPHT), quality grade (VVS1–SI2), shape (Round, Oval, Cushion, Princess, etc.), and approximate carat weight.

💡 Not sure about stone grades? VVS1 = near perfect (most expensive). VS1/VS2 = very slight inclusions (excellent value). SI1/SI2 = small inclusions (great price point, usually not visible to the naked eye).
5

Reference Image (optional but recommended)

Upload any inspiration photos — magazine cuts, screenshots, other jewelry photos. This gives the CAD designer a clear visual direction for the design. JPG, PNG, and PDF files are all accepted.

6

Special Instructions

Any additional notes: engraving text, specific design requests, allergies, preferred finishing, delivery deadline, or anything else the team should know.

✓

Order Submitted

The order is submitted and immediately appears in the system with status "Waiting Confirmation." The order is assigned a unique PO number (e.g., KJ-CUST-0042 for customer orders). The Authorizer will review it next.

✅ After placing an order, customers will see it appear immediately in their "My Orders" list with a progress timeline.

Customer Step-by-Step Guide

Log In

Go to the Kira Jewels platform URL. Enter your email address and password. You'll be taken directly to "My Orders."

2

Place an Order (if new)

Click the blue "+ Place New Order" button. Fill in the form — choose your jewelry type, metal, stone preferences, size. Optionally upload a reference photo. Click "Place Order."

💡 The more detail you provide in the Special Instructions field, the closer the first design will be to what you have in mind. Mention any specifics like thin bands, hidden halos, milgrain edges, specific stone ratios, etc.
3

Follow the Timeline

Click on any order to open its details. The timeline at the top shows each stage and highlights where your order currently is. Completed stages are shown in the order's accent color; future stages are grey.

When your design is ready, you'll see it in the "Design Files" section of your order with an "Awaiting Your Approval" status. Click the "👁 View" button to open the design in full screen. You can:

→ Request Changes — type your feedback and the designer will revise
→ Reject — if the design isn't close, a fresh start will be made

💡 Be as specific as possible when requesting changes: "Please make the band thinner" or "The prongs should be more rounded" will get you a faster revision than "not quite right."

Once you approve the design, your piece goes into manufacturing. Your order status will update as it progresses — you can check at any time to see where it is.

When your piece is shipped, a tracking number will appear in your order details (under "Order Info"). Use this to track your delivery with the courier.

Frequently Asked Questions
Common questions from customers, staff, and stakeholders — answered in plain language.

For Customers

How long does a custom jewelry order take? ▼
Typical timelines: CAD design takes 3–7 business days for the first draft. Customer review and revisions take as long as needed (usually 1–5 days per round). Manufacturing takes 3–5 weeks. Shipping takes 3–7 days. Total: approximately 6–8 weeks from order to delivery, depending on the complexity and number of design revisions.
Can I see the design before manufacturing begins? ▼
Yes — absolutely. Nothing goes to manufacturing until you have reviewed and approved the CAD design. You'll see a detailed digital rendering of your piece before any metal is cast or stones are set. If you're not happy, we revise until you are.
What if I change my mind about the design after approving? ▼
Once you've approved the design and manufacturing has begun, changes are difficult and may incur additional costs. It's important to be thorough during the CAD review stage. If you need to discuss changes after approval, contact your sales rep immediately through the order conversation.
How do I know what "Certified Lab Grown Diamond" means vs Non Certified CVD/HPHT? ▼
All three are real lab-grown diamonds with the same physical properties as natural diamonds. Certified means the stone comes with a grading certificate from a recognized lab (like GIA or IGI) confirming its exact specifications. Non Certified CVD is grown using Chemical Vapour Deposition — typically higher quality. Non Certified HPHT is grown using High Pressure High Temperature — a different process. Non-certified stones are more affordable but don't come with a certificate. Your sales rep can guide you on which is right for your budget and needs.
I can't log in — what should I do? ▼
Contact your Kira Jewels sales representative or the admin team. They can reset your password through the system. Note: for security reasons, there is no self-service password reset — a team member must assist you.

For the Internal Team

What's the difference between "Reject" and "Request Changes" on a CAD file? ▼
"Request Changes" means the design is on the right track but needs adjustments — the designer refines the same concept. "Reject" means the design doesn't meet requirements at all and a fresh approach is needed. Use "Request Changes" whenever possible as it's faster. Reserve "Reject" for when the design fundamentally misses the brief.
Who can see the quoted price / revenue data? ▼
Only the Admin role can see quoted costs and revenue figures. All other staff roles (Sales Rep, CAD Designer, Factory Manager, etc.) and customers cannot see any financial data. This is enforced at both the front-end display level and the back-end API level.
What happens if I move an order to the wrong status by mistake? ▼
Ask the Admin to move it back. Admins can set an order to any status. If you don't have admin access, message the admin team through the order conversation or directly. All status changes are logged in the conversation thread so there's a full audit trail.
Can internal messages be seen by customers? ▼
No. When posting a message, staff can toggle it to "Internal" — these messages are only visible to other staff members, not to the customer. Use this for notes about pricing, internal concerns, or team coordination that the customer shouldn't see.
How does the AI Order Brief work? ▼
Click "Generate AI Brief" (or "✨ Generate Summary") on any order detail page. The system sends the order's key information to an AI model, which writes a concise 3–5 sentence summary covering the piece type, key specs, customer details, and any special notes. This is useful for Authorizers who need to quickly review many orders. The brief is generated once and then cached so it appears instantly on future visits.

Glossary of Key Terms

PO Number
Purchase Order Number. A unique identifier for each order (e.g., KJ-CUST-0042). Used to track the order throughout the system.
CAD File
Computer-Aided Design file. A digital 3D model of the jewelry piece created by the designer showing exact dimensions and details.
SKU
Stock Keeping Unit. A unique product code assigned to each design once approved. Identifies the exact specification of the piece.
VPO
Vendor Purchase Order. A formal instruction sent to the factory to begin manufacturing a specific piece with exact specifications.
Job Bag
A physical document envelope that travels with the jewelry piece through the factory, containing all specs, the design, and tracking information.
Authorization
The act of a senior team member approving a new order to be released for design work. A quality gate before resources are committed.
Revision
A new version of a CAD design file after changes are made based on customer feedback. Labeled Rev #1, Rev #2, etc.
Pipeline Board
A visual overview showing all orders grouped by stage — like a kanban board. Lets management see the entire workload at a glance.
CVD Diamond
Chemical Vapour Deposition — a method of growing lab diamonds using carbon-rich gas. Produces very high quality stones.
HPHT Diamond
High Pressure High Temperature — the original method of growing lab diamonds, mimicking natural formation conditions underground.
VVS / VS / SI
Diamond clarity grades. VVS (Very Very Slightly Included) = near flawless. VS = Very Slight inclusions. SI = Slight Inclusions. The scale goes VVS1 → VVS2 → VS1 → VS2 → SI1 → SI2.
Internal Message
A message in the order conversation that is visible only to staff, not to the customer. Used for team coordination and internal notes.
